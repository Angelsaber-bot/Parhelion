# BlackSail1718 — historical art notes

Research checked 2026-10-27. This is an evidence-informed fantasy chapter, **not a reconstruction of Nassau or a replica of Queen Anne’s Revenge (QAR)**. Preserve the existing routes, star fort, floating wrecks, skull reef, ghost fleet and windmill. Archaeological evidence supports material/detail choices, not the fantasy geography.

## Ships: evidence → art direction

- **Hull:** QAR preserves only a small section of framing and bottom planking, not a complete silhouette. [NC QAR Project: ship components](https://www.qaronline.org/conservation/artifacts/ship-components). Its frames, hull planks and anchor stocks are oak; replaceable outer sacrificial planks are pine. [Sacrificial planking](https://www.qaronline.org/conservation/artifacts/ship-components/sacrificial-planking). **Art inference:** broad wood strakes, dark seams, timber ribs exposed in wrecks, and a separately readable weathered lower sheathing band. Do not substitute shiny copper sheathing or claim an exact hull reconstruction.
- **Cordage:** recovered rope varies in size and is mostly hemp. [Rope](https://www.qaronline.org/conservation/artifacts/ship-components/rope). **Art inference:** distinguish heavy supporting shrouds/stays from thinner running lines; use matte, fibrous brown/tan rope rather than identical thin black wires everywhere.
- **Deadeyes:** a conserved QAR ash deadeye has three lanyard holes and pitch staining; the assemblage includes iron straps and chainplates. [Rigging elements, 2023](https://www.qaronline.org/blog/2023-02-01/artifact-month-deadeye). **Art inference:** paired dark wooden discs and short lanyards at lower shrouds, with iron attachments against the hull. The article’s introductory rigging photograph is explicitly a **Batavia reconstruction**, not QAR.
- **Sails:** QAR’s small heavy-sail fragments show half-basket weave. [Sail fragments](https://www.qaronline.org/conservation/artifacts/ship-components/sail-fragments). **Art inference:** coarse woven surface, subdued off-white/tan, panel seams, tensioned corners and selective repairs. These colors, repairs and complete sail plan are artistic choices, not established by the fragments. Black/ghost sails remain fantasy narrative treatments.
- **Cannon carriage:** the project describes early-18th-century naval carriages with two timber cheeks, a bed, solid axles/truck wheels, a breech-elevating quoin wedge and iron capsquares securing trunnions. QAR evidence is fragmentary hardware, rope and a small wood remnant. [Gun carriage](https://www.qaronline.org/conservation/artifacts/arms-and-armament/gun-carriage). **Art inference:** low compact carriages, solid small wheels, a wedge beneath the breech, iron straps and recoil ropes—not tall field-gun carriages with large spoked wheels.
- **Cannon and ports:** the QAR page’s **November 2015 inventory** lists varied cannon sizes and English/Swedish origins, with manufacture approximately 1640–1714. [Cannon](https://www.qaronline.org/conservation/artifacts/arms-and-armament/cannon). **Art inference:** restrained size variation, reinforcement rings, trunnions and dark recessed gunports. Exact gunport count, spacing, lids and ship-wide armament layout are not verified here.
- **Period comparison, not identity:** Royal Museums Greenwich’s SLR0398 is a contemporary **c.1710** fully rigged 20-gun sixth-rate model with early-18th-century rigging. [Museum object record](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-66359). Use only to understand period relationships of hull, spars and rigging; it is not QAR. A model’s listed materials do not establish the full-size vessel’s materials.

## Nassau buildings and fort chronology

- The official Bahamas timeline dates **Fort Nassau to 1697**, **Fort Montagu construction to 1741–1742**, the **main part of Fort Charlotte’s completion to 1789**, and **Fort Fincastle to 1793**. [Government timeline](https://www.bahamas.gov.bs/wps/wcm/connect/df7f2c36-598d-4218-bdd6-898cdcd2816f/Bahamas%2BTimeline.pdf?MOD=AJPERES). None of the latter three should be portrayed as a literal 1718 landmark. Keep the game’s star fort expressly fictional.
- Montagu’s official listing identifies **local limestone**, but this is a **1741** structure, not direct evidence for the game’s 1718 fort. [Fort Montagu](https://www.bahamas.com/plan-your-trip/things-to-do/fort-montagu). **Art inference:** pale rough masonry, broad low ramparts, cannon embrasures, worn capstones and iron hardware. “Coral stone” should not be presented as a verified material identification.
- The Department of Archives-backed listing says the stone Deanery is **believed to date to about 1710**, with chamfered quoins, three storeys, originally tiered verandas and a separate kitchen containing a fireplace and domed brick oven. Renovations are acknowledged. [Deanery House](https://www.bahamas.com/plan-your-trip/things-to-do/the-deanery-house). This is a qualified analogue, not proof that every visible detail existed in 1718.
- The Anglican diocese records wooden Nassau church buildings before the **1703 destruction**, another wooden church in **1724**, and a cut-stone replacement in **1754**. [Christ Church history](https://bahtcianglican.org/find-us/christ-church-cathedral/). Do not project the present Gothic cathedral backward into 1718.
- **Art inference, not verified building plans:** the port/tavern/warehouse ensemble can use modest timber frames, broad weathered boards, dark iron hinges, plain shutters, loading doors, casks, rope coils, restrained limewashed masonry and occasional chamfered quoins. Hanging tavern signs, lean-to roofs, post braces and cargo arrangements are authored storytelling. No verified 1718 Nassau tavern or warehouse elevation was established in this pass; avoid claims of exact authenticity or copying later picturesque colonial facades.

## Lighthouse: pre-modern light, not a modern optic

- Trinity House dates Rudyerd’s **conical wooden Eddystone tower to 1709**; its candle-lit lantern burned in **1755**. Smeaton’s stone successor dates to **1759**. [Eddystone history](https://www.trinityhouse.co.uk/lighthouses-and-lightvessels/eddystone-lighthouse).
- National Records of Scotland describes early metal coal-fire enclosures and the **1635 Isle of May** light as a stone structure with a coal fire. [The Dark Coast](https://nrscotland.gov.uk/learning-and-events/for-the-safety-of-all-scotlands-lighthouses/the-dark-coast/).
- **Art inference:** use a modest iron brazier/cage with ember glow and smoke, or a small framed candle lantern. Avoid a conspicuous Fresnel lens and clean rotating searchlight. These are European period analogues, **not evidence of a Nassau lighthouse in 1718**. Preserve the existing gameplay landmark while making its lighting vocabulary less modern.

## Asset and evidence limits

No museum photographs, copyrighted textures or third-party 3D models are imported by this research. Links are references; implement original geometry/materials. Do not describe modern replicas, conserved fragments or later buildings as exact 1718 originals. Supernatural light, floating ships and skull forms remain clearly intentional fantasy.
