# Kyoto: historical references and art direction

This is a playable, geographically compressed fantasy interpretation of Kyoto, with the Honnō-ji incident of 1582 as its narrative anchor. It is **not an archaeological reconstruction**. Requested iconic sights from different periods are deliberately combined as time-layer anomalies. In particular, the dense Senbon Torii landscape and several familiar surviving temple forms must not be presented as their exact 1582 appearance.

## Reference distinctions

- **Fushimi Inari / Senbon Torii:** vermilion painted timber, dark lower post protection, repeated gateways framing a clear mountain path. The shrine dates the custom of donating these gates to the Edo period, after 1582. [Official shrine description](https://inari.jp/en/map/spot_07/).
- **Kinkaku-ji:** distinguish the timber/light lower floor from the gilded upper floors, layered roof silhouettes, shaded window bays and the phoenix finial. The temple also notes that the grounds were rearranged in the early Edo period. [Official precinct guide](https://www.shokoku-ji.jp/en/kinkakuji/guide/).
- **Kiyomizu-dera:** the hillside setting, large timber stage and exposed support structure are the principal silhouette references. The temple dates most of the surviving buildings to reconstruction in 1633; these are visual references, not proof of the exact 1582 structure. [Official history and main-hall guide](https://www.kiyomizudera.or.jp/en/learn/).
- **Honnō-ji:** the incident site is not interchangeable with the temple's present precinct. The game retains its existing quest geography rather than claiming accurate city-scale coordinates. [Official temple history](https://kyoto-honnouji.jp/en/pages/about), [Kyoto City historical-site record](https://www2.city.kyoto.lg.jp/html/somu/rekishi/fm/ishibumi/html/na149.html).
- **Arashiyama / Tenryū-ji:** the new district uses a timber bridge, mountain river, bamboo approach and pond/rock garden inspired by the area. The bridge, hall and garden composition are authored abstractions, not surveyed replicas of present structures. [Nara National Research Institute garden record](https://www.nabunken.go.jp/org/bunka/jgd/pages/GardenAtTenryuji.html).
- **Sannenzaka and the central city:** retain the hillside approach and street network, but vary trade fronts, roof massing, courtyard compounds and service details. The street art is a fantasy composite, not a claim that every modern preserved frontage existed in 1582. The sixteenth-century Rakuchū Rakugai screens are a useful near-period reference for urban variety. [Yonezawa City Uesugi Museum](https://www.denkoku-no-mori.yonezawa.yamagata.jp/rakuchyu_rakugai.htm).

## Visual rules

Readable anime-style silhouettes take priority over indiscriminate geometric density. Roofs, timber joints, storefront lattices, awnings, veranda supports and thresholds provide structural detail. Wood, plaster, ceramic, stone, fabric, bamboo and gold use distinct restrained material responses. Gold is not an emissive yellow surface. Building variation should change geometry and use, not merely recolor a cloned box.

Main traversal corridors, contract markers and existing objectives remain the gameplay contract. Decorative geometry should not create invisible collision traps. Added district routes require exported-collision checks, not only source-coordinate inspection.

The geometry and surface treatment are authored in this repository. Linked site photographs and text were used as research references; they are not redistributed as textures or other game assets. Existing third-party dependencies and character assets retain their separate notices.
