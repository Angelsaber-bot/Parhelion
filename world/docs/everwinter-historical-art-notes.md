# Everwinter / Parhelion 1812 — historical art notes

Research date: 2026-10-27. Scope: architectural silhouettes, material differentiation and fieldworks for a stylized Russia-1812 map.

**This is a historically informed gameplay abstraction, not a surveyed reconstruction or a claim that the prototype is historically perfect.** Moscow landmarks and the later Berezina retreat are geographically and chronologically separate; juxtaposition compresses the campaign. Snow coverage, dimensions, placement, distress and palette are art direction unless explicitly documented below. Modern monument photographs are shape references, not proof of their exact 1812 finish. No reference image or unlicensed texture was downloaded or incorporated.

## Moscow landmark hierarchy

- **Spasskaya / Kremlin gate towers:** Kremlin Museums dates the principal tower to 1491 and its multi-tiered, tented upper stage to 1624–25. It identifies white-stone carved arched decoration, turrets and pyramids. **Art inference:** a broad square masonry gate base, distinct clock/tier zone, narrowing tented roof and small period-appropriate finial reads differently from a church dome. Vary tower widths and heights rather than repeating one miniature onion church. Do not add Soviet red stars. [Official monument history](https://kremlin-architectural-ensemble.kreml.ru/en-Us/architecture/view/spasskaya-bashnya-moskovskogo-kremlya/).
- **Walls:** a Presidential Library catalogue preserves a river-front plan/elevation showing standing and destroyed Kremlin walls/towers after 1812, dated no later than 1815. It is a stronger period-layout lead than today's skyline. **Art inference:** crenellated wall rhythm, substantial masonry mass and projecting towers; localized exposure/weathering instead of uniformly bright new brick. The [UNESCO-hosted Kremlin management plan](https://whc.unesco.org/document/194954), printed p. 108, records a 1680 order to whitewash the fortress. Worn pale limewashed planes over exposed brick are therefore a stronger period cue than uniformly saturated red. Exact 1812 coverage remains unverified; a pristine uniform white finish is not asserted. (Indexed text retrieved; full PDF exceeds the web reader's size limit.) [Archival drawing record, RGIA provenance](https://www.prlib.ru/item/389740).
- **Ivan the Great:** official history records an octagonal tower built 1505–08, heightened under Boris Godunov with a tall drum and gilded dome to 81 m. The central tower survived the 1812 demolition attempt; the ensemble was reconstructed in 1814–15. **Art inference:** emphasize slender, diminishing octagonal tiers, dark bell openings, pale mineral surfaces and one restrained gold crown. Do not copy the post-restoration flanking ensemble as an exact pre-fire model. [Kremlin Museums](https://ivan-the-great-bell-tower.kreml.ru/en-Us/history/view/kolokolnya-ivan-velikiy/).
- **St Basil's:** the State Historical Museum dates the cathedral to 1555–61 and describes its multiple churches and later historical layers. **Art inference:** a tightly clustered, asymmetrical group of different-height drums and onion silhouettes around a central tented mass, with red/ivory structural contrast and selective muted color. The exact dome-by-dome 1812 pigment/pattern scheme is **unverified**; modern candy-bright finishes are not a dated 1812 color chart. [Official museum](https://en.shm.ru/museum/hvb/).

**Tsar Bell correction:** the monumental bell remained in its casting pit until it was raised in 1836. Do not display it on its modern above-ground pedestal in an 1812 scene. **Art inference:** a fenced depression with a partly visible bell can preserve landmark discovery without claiming exact excavation geometry. [Kremlin Museums](https://www.kreml.ru/en-Us/visit-to-kremlin/what-to-see/tsar-kolokol/).

## Ordinary city fabric, not repeated palaces

The State Public Historical Library reproduces S. M. Lyubetsky's 1872 retrospective account: 1812 Moscow had both masonry and timber houses and shops, with substantially more timber houses. The numerical account is retrospective, not a contemporary measured plan. [Library exhibit](https://vv1812.shpl.ru/moscow.html) (search-index text available; direct page retrieval encountered a redirect loop).

Moscow's official heritage assessment documents Sytin's house as an 1804 survivor of the fire. This supplies a specific pre-fire domestic counterweight to monumental masonry. [Heritage assessment](https://www.mos.ru/upload/documents/files/6879/AKTSitinskii5ot030618sprotokolami.pdf).

**Art inference:** mix low timber/plastered houses, masonry bases, restrained classical masonry frontages and merchant yards. Differentiate wall plaster from exposed brick, soot from dark timber, and roof snow from pale stone. Vary bay counts, eaves, shutters, gables/hips, attached service wings and court gaps. Use restrained brick reds, chalk/ochre plaster, aged brown timber and dark roofs; avoid one atlas/color and identical roofline across every district. Distressed patches should follow eaves, bases, openings and fire damage, not random camouflage.

**Exclude later landmarks:** today's GUM opened in 1893 ([GUM's own history](https://gum.ru/history/)); the present Grand Kremlin Palace dates to 1838–49 ([Kremlin Museums](https://old.kreml.ru/learning/lectures/cikl-6-2018/)); Minin–Pozharsky monument is 1818 ([Moscow heritage record](https://www.mos.ru/upload/documents/files/3059/PamyatnikMininyiPojarskomy.pdf)). Also exclude Lenin's mausoleum and Soviet iconography. Do not use a later rebuilding of a trading-row frontage as evidence of its pre-fire design.

## Berezina: timber trestles, not modern pontoons

A French Army historical publication hosted by the Ministry of Armed Forces' heritage service explicitly describes **two trestle bridges**, improvised from demolished-house wood. Infantry/cavalry decking used old boards and bark; artillery decking used round timber. The word *pontonniers* names the specialists and does **not** establish floating boats under these particular bridges. [Official-hosted publication, printed p. 7](https://www.cheminsdememoire.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/2022-08/armees_revolution_empire.pdf) (indexed text retrieved; direct fetch returned HTTP 502).

**Art inference:** timber bents/trestle legs descend into the river, longitudinal bearers support irregular transverse decking; distinguish a lighter crossing from a heavier wagon/artillery crossing. Modest sag, displaced boards, wet dark lower timbers and repairs communicate emergency construction. Do not use steel Bailey trusses, modular metal floats or a row of identical support boats. Exact joinery, dimensions and bridge separation are not asserted here.

## Field headquarters and bivouac

Musée de l'Armée describes Napoleon's headquarters as several tents joined by canvas corridors, not permanently erected. When time did not permit, officers used requisitioned houses/farms and soldiers slept outdoors under haversack blankets. [Museum teaching guide, room 29](https://www.musee-armee.fr/fileadmin/user_upload/Documents/Ressources_pedagogiques_-_nouvelles/De_Louis_XIV_a_Napoleon/Parcours_Ingres-Napoleon_BAT-2.pdf).

Mobilier national catalogues a surviving tent wall, inventory GMTC 476, design date 1812, material linen/textile. [Official collection record](https://collection.mobilier-national.fr/objet/GMTC-476-000).

**Art inference:** muted undyed/aged canvas, tension seams, ropes and wooden stakes; a small distinct command cluster, uneven outdoor bivouac groups, blankets, restrained wooden baggage and wagon silhouettes. No modern camouflage, nylon dome tents, plastic equipment or uniform luxury tent city. These are general period cues, not proof of a specific tent layout during the retreat.
