# Arthur Kingdom and Parhelion Valley Art Direction

Research checked 27 October 2026. This is a repository implementation brief for the two remaining destination refinements, not a claim that either fictional destination reconstructs a real place. The recommendations below are original art direction. Primary sources establish the historical and scientific boundaries; they do not prescribe the fictional buildings.

## Arthur Kingdom

### Historical boundary

English Heritage distinguishes the small rectangular buildings of Tintagel's flourishing fifth to seventh century settlement from the castle built in the 1230s. The site's Arthurian literary association appears in the twelfth century. These are separate historical layers, not evidence of a sixth century turreted Camelot. [English Heritage history of Tintagel](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/tintagel-castle/history-and-legend/history/)

Richard of Cornwall's later castle was itself probably motivated by the legends. That makes a romance-inspired castle a suitable imaginative reference, provided the game calls it legendary rather than an archaeological reconstruction. [English Heritage on Richard of Cornwall and Arthur](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/tintagel-castle/history-and-legend/richard-of-cornwall)

### Visual decision

Build a legendary sunset Camelot above a more modest British settlement. The principal silhouette should be an asymmetric stone castle group: one tall hall, two or three round towers of unequal height, connected crenellated walls, and restrained pointed roof caps. Grouped masses and their negative spaces must remain legible before any tiny ornament is visible.

- **Grounded lower layer:** small rectangular rough-stone halls, limewashed wall patches, timber porches, low enclosure walls, and stepped cliff terraces. The rectangular building footprints have a source basis; their completed roofs, porches, finishes, and arrangement are interpretation.
- **Romance upper layer:** honey-colored castle walls, slate-blue or weathered copper caps, carved entrance lintels, long wine-red pennons, and a sheltered ceremonial courtyard. These are deliberate fantasy choices, not dated sixth century features.
- **Material craft:** use large calm stone faces, a few exposed irregular masonry courses at edges, deep arch recesses, darker stone plinths, and fine roof seams. Place detail where it explains construction rather than uniformly scattering noise.
- **Anime lighting:** amber sunset rim light against cool violet shadow planes; desaturated limestone rather than metallic yellow. Keep the shadow side readable. A few warm windows should accent the entrance and hall, not turn every opening into a glowing grid.
- **Landmark hierarchy:** castle first, low hall cluster second, bridge or gate third. A heraldic cloth or ceremonial sword motif can establish the fiction without adding unrelated architectural languages.

Do not reuse Kyoto's layered eaves, Russian Winter's onion-domed silhouette, or Nassau's colonial shuttered facades. Do not label this an authentic sixth century castle. Suitable wording is “a legendary Arthurian kingdom with early medieval British cues.”

## Parhelion Valley

### Scientific boundary

DOE ARM facilities use combinations of cloud radars, lidar, radiometers, weather balloons, cameras, and operational shelters. Their recognizable functions provide a better basis for a contemporary research destination than an arbitrary glowing tower. [DOE ARM Mobile Facility fact sheet](https://armgov.svcs.arm.gov/publications/fact-sheets/docs/doe-sc-arm-15-022.pdf)

The ARM Eastern North Atlantic observatory provides a useful spatial precedent: a radar group and a separate instrument field with clear observational exposure. This is a planning reference, not a model to copy or a claim that the fictional valley is in the Azores. [ARM observatory background](https://lasso-ena.svcs.arm.gov/latest/background.html)

Sun dogs are bright patches approximately 22 degrees to either side of the Sun, with red toward it and blue farther outward. The effect comes from sunlight interacting with atmospheric ice crystals. The visual motif should therefore read as paired light near the Sun, not as planets or an aurora. [National Weather Service explanation](https://www.weather.gov/arx/why_halos_sundogs_pillars)

Ice crystals in high cloud can cause this phenomenon; a snow-covered foreground is not required. That permits a green or tawny valley to stay visually separate from Russian Winter. [Met Office optical effects](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/weather/optical-effects)

### Visual decision

Create an original contemporary research campus appropriate to a fictional 2026 setting. Use low laboratory bars, one modest observation volume, and a small number of legible instruments. The architecture is a design proposal rather than a replica of a named observatory or an assertion about a real 2026 construction project.

- **Campus silhouette:** asymmetric cream or pale concrete bars, charcoal recessed glazing, teal standing-seam roofs, visible structural braces, and a sheltered glass entrance. A short connector or skybridge adds depth without creating a generic city skyline.
- **Instrument silhouettes:** an angled parabolic cloud-radar dish; a small hemispherical all-sky camera housing; a slender wind-sensor mast; and a low upright lidar or ceilometer enclosure with a clear top aperture. These should differ in size, height, orientation, and placement. Shapes are stylized rather than engineering drawings.
- **Functional siting:** place the sensor garden in an open patch, not under a roof or directly against a tall wall. Keep a believable service path and clearance around the radar. Separate the public arrival court from the instrument zone.
- **Material craft:** visible panel seams, restrained ribbing, brushed alloy rails, darker base flashings, and occasional service labels. Use warm interior illumination at the entrance against cool exterior surfaces.
- **Landscape:** pale rock, green meadow or tawny grass, a narrow reflective stream, and broad sky. A few protected planting beds can soften the campus without obscuring instruments.
- **Sky motif:** a restrained central Sun with softer lateral flares and, optionally, a faint halo. If camera framing compresses the geometry, treat it as stylized imagery rather than a calibrated atmospheric simulation. Avoid neon rainbow bands, permanent aurora curtains, or arbitrary extra suns.

Do not reuse castle battlements, pagoda tiers, onion domes, pirate-port balconies, or an all-over snow palette. Do not use a glowing obelisk as a substitute for research architecture. The radar, camera, mast, and low laboratory ensemble should communicate the destination even with all emissive effects disabled.

## Shared acceptance checks

1. In a plain silhouette or grayscale view, Arthur reads as a clustered stone stronghold and Valley as low contemporary laboratories with instrument structures.
2. Each destination has one dominant landmark, a secondary building group, and a clear arrival route. Foreground props do not compete with the landmark.
3. The two new destinations cannot be mistaken for Kyoto, Russian Winter, or Nassau merely because the same house mesh has changed color.
4. Material details reveal construction: stone joints and roof seams for Arthur; panel divisions, glazing recesses, and instrument mounts for Valley.
5. Warm and cool planes separate forms without hiding the playable route. Accent lighting remains subordinate to geometry.
6. Interface and lore text keep Arthurian fantasy separate from historical fact and the invented Valley campus separate from real scientific institutions.
7. No external images, models, logos, or copyrighted reconstruction drawings are required by this brief. References are for research, not permission to redistribute assets.
